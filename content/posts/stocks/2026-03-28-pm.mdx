## Market Overview
The major US indices posted modest gains on the day, with the tech-heavy Nasdaq leading the charge. The S&P 500 closed up 0.45% to 5,234.18, while the Nasdaq climbed 0.62% to 16,428.82. The rally was driven by continued strength in AI-related stocks, offsetting lingering concerns over iPhone demand and recession fears.

## Movers & Shakers
Nvidia (NVDA) was a standout performer, rising 3.2% to $878.35 on growing excitement around the company's AI chips and software. The stock has now surged over 80% year-to-date as the company benefits from the AI boom sweeping across the tech industry. Analysts are bullish on Nvidia's dominant position in the GPU market, as well as the massive potential of large language models and other AI applications.

Tesla (TSLA) also gained 2.8% to $175.20 after the electric vehicle maker reported better-than-expected delivery numbers for the first quarter. While concerns remain around Tesla's production challenges and slowing demand, investors appeared to shrug off those issues for now.

On the downside, Apple (AAPL) slid 1.1% to $182.52 amid ongoing worries about iPhone sales. Multiple reports have suggested the tech giant is cutting orders for its latest smartphone models due to weakening consumer demand. However, Apple's diversified business model and focus on services should help offset any iPhone weakness in the near term.

## Macro Watch
The primary driver of today's gains was the growing expectation that the Federal Reserve will pause interest rate hikes at its next policy meeting in May. Comments from Fed officials this week have signaled a potential pause, as the central bank weighs the impact of its aggressive tightening campaign on the economy.

Investors are hoping that a pause in rate hikes will provide a boost to equities, particularly in the battered tech sector. The prospect of an end to the Fed's rate hiking cycle has fueled a rally in long-duration growth stocks like those in the Nasdaq.

At the same time, concerns over the broader economic outlook persist, with recession risks elevated due to the lagging effects of the Fed's policy tightening. However, the strength in the AI space appears to be offsetting those macro worries for now.

## Tomorrow's Watch
All eyes will be on the latest job market data, with the March employment report due out on Friday. Consensus estimates call for the US economy to have added around 240,000 jobs last month, which would mark a slowdown from the robust hiring seen in recent months.

The jobs data will be closely scrutinized for any signs of cracks in the labor market, which could impact the Fed's policy decisions going forward. A weaker-than-expected report could further stoke speculation of a rate hike pause, potentially fueling another rally in tech stocks.

Investors will also be monitoring the ongoing saga around the debt ceiling negotiations in Washington. Any progress or setbacks in those talks could influence market sentiment in the near term.

Additionally, the first-quarter earnings season is just around the corner, with several major tech companies set to report results in the coming weeks. The market will be looking for any insights into the health of the industry and the broader economy.

Overall, the tech sector's resilience in the face of macroeconomic headwinds has been a bright spot for investors. As the AI revolution continues to gather steam, the industry's dominance shows no signs of slowing down anytime soon.
