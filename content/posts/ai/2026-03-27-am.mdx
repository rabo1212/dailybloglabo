## Lead Story
In a major legal victory for Anthropic, a US district judge has issued a preliminary injunction blocking the Pentagon's effort to designate the AI company as a "supply chain risk." This comes after Anthropic sued the Department of Defense, alleging that the labeling was politically motivated and would unfairly restrict the company's ability to work with the US government. 

The judge found that Anthropic was likely to succeed on the merits of its claim that the Pentagon's actions were "arbitrary and capricious" under the Administrative Procedure Act. The ruling cites concerns over the lack of a clear process or justification for the supply chain risk designation. This marks a significant setback for the DoD's crackdown on Chinese-linked AI firms, which has faced growing scrutiny and legal challenges.

The injunction is a vindication for Anthropic, which has positioned itself as a US-based, values-aligned alternative to large language models from tech giants. With this ruling, the company can continue vying for lucrative government contracts without the stigma of a "supply chain risk" label. It also sets an important precedent around the limits of the government's national security powers when it comes to regulating the AI industry.
