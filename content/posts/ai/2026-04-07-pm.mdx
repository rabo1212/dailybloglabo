## Lead Story

The recent research from USC revealing that AI may be "making us think and write more alike" raises unsettling questions about the future of human cognition and expression. By training on vast troves of online text, powerful language models like GPT-3 are unconsciously shaping how we communicate, converging us towards a narrow band of acceptable discourse.

The study found that as people interact with AI assistants, their writing becomes more similar to the model's output - using the same vocabulary, sentence structures, and even subtle biases. This "alignment" effect persists even after the interaction, as users internalize the model's linguistic patterns. Essentially, AI is molding our thinking to fit its own modes of expression.

This has profound implications. If AI is unconsciously homogenizing how we reason and articulate ideas, it could stifle the diversity of human thought and creativity that has historically driven progress. An ecology of diverse perspectives and communication styles is crucial for innovation - but what happens when we all start sounding the same? 

The problem is exacerbated by the fact that language models are themselves trained on biased data, inheriting the prejudices and limitations of online discourse. As these models set the new baseline for "normal" communication, they risk cementing those biases and blindspots into the cultural mainstream.

Worryingly, this process may be invisible and self-reinforcing. As people's writing converges towards the AI-influenced norm, that norm itself shifts, creating a moving target that's difficult to track or resist. We may be sleepwalking into an AI-shaped future without even realizing it.
